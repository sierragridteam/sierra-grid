# Crossing — between foothill and high country

Crossing is a place-to-place observational transect. Move a slider or select a
named stop from Murphys toward Ebbetts Pass, pause, and read what the sky, ground,
roads, utilities, fire authorities and radio observers report there. An arbitrary
place lens and regional/unlocated shelf keep the five-stop invitation from hiding
the rest of the public data.

This is not a computed driving route or a safety assessment. The connecting line
is an index of named places, not a measured route geometry. A live evidence sentence
contrasts the place's hazard posture with its independently computed roads severity.
The distinction itself can be the discovery: an overall quiet posture does not
mean open roads. Weather observations lead into the corresponding forecast; burn
records open both authorities; current events lead into archived changes.

Each observation opens a source-aware evidence reading with meaningful field groups,
clocks, quantities, original source text and links. URL state preserves place,
domain, collection, historical filters, pagination depth and selected snapshots.
Empty or failed evidence is a limit of the reading, never a story of safety.

The visual language uses Sierra's parchment, forest ink and brass, a wide contour-
patterned transect, oversized Newsreader typography and compact marginal indexing.
Shared Sierra tokens, logo, controls and time-series primitives carry the identity.
The decorative contours do not represent measured topography.

## Alternatives considered before interaction implementation

1. **Fieldnotes:** a question-led editorial journal; six equal chapters would lead
   readers from live sentences into specimens and revisions.
2. **Crossing (selected by root):** geographic movement changes the lens through
   which every domain is read, with a regional shelf for unlocated evidence.
3. **Observation Desk:** pin two live evidence types side by side, then follow
   shared place/time/source relationships without implying causation.

A preliminary Fieldnotes shell was replaced with Crossing's geographic interaction.
Typography and the secondary evidence index remain useful reading supports.
